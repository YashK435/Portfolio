Overview

Game Overview

Hello Owner places the player in an abandoned warehouse for a late-night job unloading parcels. What begins as routine labor slowly escalates into a supernatural trap tied to ownership, greed, and inherited guilt.

The game deliberately avoids combat. Horror emerges through environmental control, audio-driven tension, and rule-based systems that strip player agency — not through jumpscares or monsters. The warehouse itself is the antagonist.

Built end-to-end as a solo project in Unreal Engine, Hello Owner demonstrates narrative-driven system design, audio-centric gameplay, and a complete start-to-finish development pipeline.

Gameplay

Core Loop

Receive Objective → Perform Warehouse Task → Environment Reacts → Audio & Visual Anomalies Escalate → Explore to Progress → Decisions Influence Ending

This loop repeats with rising psychological pressure, not mechanical difficulty. The player's fear comes from familiar tasks becoming hostile, not from skill-based challenge.

Design

Design Pillars

01

Normalcy Before Horror

The experience begins grounded and believable. Routine warehouse labor establishes trust before the environment breaks it — making disruptions feel genuinely unsettling rather than expected.

02

Audio-First Fear

Sound communicates danger, timing, and progression more than visuals. Directional whispers, electrical hums near objectives, and intentional silence are primary fear tools. Players who listen survive longer.

03

Environment as Antagonist

The warehouse actively reconfigures, blocks paths, and enforces its own rules. Racks shift during darkness. Lights dictate movement. The space itself decides whether you're worthy of escape.

04

No Combat, Only Consequences

The player cannot fight back. Survival depends entirely on observation, restraint, and understanding the maze's rules. Mechanical skill is irrelevant — psychological endurance is everything.

Mechanics

Rule-Based Maze System

The maze is not a puzzle to solve — it is a test the warehouse imposes. Triggered after the Truck 3 sequence, the entire environment reconfigures under red, unnatural lighting.

⚡

Lighting Rule System

Lights ON (5s): Player may move freely. Lights OFF (3s): Complete stillness required. Movement during darkness triggers immediate ghost capture and respawn at checkpoint.

◈

Dynamic Rack System

Warehouse racks are movable actors that shift positions during lights-off phases — blocking or revealing routes. The maze is semi-scripted: core paths are fixed, minor variations prevent memorization.

🔑

Maze Objectives

Players must locate the Office Key, discover documents revealing the warehouse's history, find the Electric Room Key, restore power, and uncover a hidden path behind a sliding cupboard.

📦

Box Mechanic

Boxes begin as tools of mundane labor — pick up, place, count. By the late game, they transform into environmental obstacles, chase elements, and narrative punishment tools. Labor becomes symbolic.

Narrative

Three Endings

Player behavior, exploration choices, and compliance during the maze sequence determine which ending unfolds. Each reinforces the themes of ownership, greed, and the cost of staying.

ENDING 01

Framed

Leaving without choosing a door. The warehouse lets you go — but the consequences of what happened here follow you out. You escape the building, not the guilt.

ENDING 02

The New Owner

Accepting ownership and repeating the cycle. The warehouse found what it was looking for. You are no longer an employee — you are the next warden of this trap.

ENDING 03 — TRUE

Burn It Down

Destroying the warehouse and breaking the cycle entirely. The hardest path. The only one that ends the story rather than continuing it. Freedom through destruction.

Reflection

Postmortem

What Went Right

  • Strong atmosphere with minimal explicit horror mechanics
  • Complete end-to-end solo pipeline from concept to final build
  • Lighting rule system created genuine psychological pressure
  • Audio design successfully drove tension without visual reliance
  • Three distinct endings with clear thematic differentiation

What Could Improve

  • Mid-game interaction variety could be expanded before the maze
  • Environmental consequences for player choices outside the maze
  • AI-generated voice acting limits emotional delivery
  • More mid-game Truck 2 tension events to better pace the escalation
